Fun things that make Grandma’s house special

  1. A simple family game

    Choose something quick to learn that can come out after dinner or on a rainy afternoon. For younger children, matching, bingo-style and picture games are usually easier than games with long rules.

  2. A small craft bin

    Keep washable markers, crayons, stickers, construction paper, child-safe scissors and glue sticks together in one handled bin. It is much easier than searching the house every time someone asks to make something.

  3. A few books that live only at Grandma’s

    Let each grandchild pick one or two favorites. Reading the same book on repeat may not sound exciting to adults, but that repetition can become part of what makes visiting Nana feel familiar.

  4. A puzzle or building set

    A puzzle, magnetic tiles or age-appropriate blocks work well because kids can use them independently or together with Grandma.

  5. Simple outdoor toys

    Sidewalk chalk, bubbles, a soft ball or a jump rope take little storage space and can turn a short visit into outdoor time without planning a special outing.

Practical items parents will appreciate

  1. Kid-friendly cups, plates and utensils

    You only need a couple of sturdy sets. Choose items that are easy to wash and appropriate for the children’s ages.

  2. A small snack basket

    Keep a few parent-approved shelf-stable snacks on hand. Ask about allergies and preferences first, then rotate items so they do not sit forgotten in the pantry.

  3. Basic toiletries

    A spare toothbrush, toothpaste, hairbrush and gentle soap can save a late-night trip to the store during an unexpected sleepover.

  4. A washable throw blanket

    Useful for movie time, naps, blanket forts and chilly mornings. Pick something soft that can go straight into the washing machine.

  5. A night-light

    Even children who sleep in the dark at home may feel differently in an unfamiliar hallway. A small plug-in night-light can make bathroom trips easier.

For sleepovers

  1. Spare pajamas

    Keep one simple set in a current size if sleepovers happen often. Because kids grow quickly, check the fit every few months instead of buying several sets.

  2. An extra pillow and washable bedding

    Nothing fancy is required. A clean pillow, fitted sheet and blanket stored together make spontaneous sleepovers much easier.

  3. A quiet bedtime activity

    A coloring book, sticker book or short chapter book gives everyone a calmer transition after a busy day.

For older grandkids

  1. A phone charging cable

    For teens, the most appreciated item may be the simplest one. Keep a commonly used charging cable in the guest room or living area so nobody has to borrow yours.

  2. A place that feels like theirs

    This can be one drawer, one basket or a shelf where they keep a book, sketchpad, game or small personal item. The point is not the stuff — it is the feeling that there is a little space at Grandma’s house that belongs to them.

Do not buy everything at once.

Start with the five items that match how your grandkids actually spend time at your house. A toddler who visits for afternoons needs a different setup than a 12-year-old who stays overnight.

What should Grandma keep for a first visit?

If you want the shortest possible list, start with a game or activity, a favorite snack, a cup or plate, one book and a blanket. That is enough to make the house feel prepared without turning a spare room into a toy store.
