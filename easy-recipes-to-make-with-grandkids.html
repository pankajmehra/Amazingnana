🍪 Cook together

12 easy recipes to make with grandkids

Choose recipes with a few safe, repeatable jobs for little helpers: measuring, pouring, stirring, rolling, decorating and setting the timer.

BakingBreakfastNo-bakeKid-helper friendly

Cooking with grandkids works best when the goal is time together, not a perfect-looking finished dish. Pick a recipe that matches their age, give each child a real job and expect the process to take a little longer than usual.

More easy recipe ideas for Grandma days

4. Mini English-muffin pizzas

Split English muffins, spread with pizza sauce, add shredded cheese and let each child choose a few toppings. Bake until the cheese is melted and the edges are crisp.

5. Yogurt-and-fruit parfaits

Layer yogurt, berries or sliced fruit and a small amount of granola in clear cups. This is a simple option when you want a kitchen activity without cooking.

6. Banana “sushi” rolls

Spread peanut butter or sunflower-seed butter on a tortilla, place a banana on top, roll it tightly and slice into bite-size rounds. Adjust for allergies and age.

7. Homemade trail-mix cups

Set out a few age-appropriate ingredients such as cereal, pretzels, dried fruit and chocolate chips. Let each grandchild build a cup. Avoid choking hazards for younger children.

8. Cinnamon-sugar toast sticks

Brush bread lightly with melted butter, sprinkle with cinnamon sugar, toast until crisp and cut into strips. Serve with fruit for an easy snack.

9. Fruit kabobs

Older children can thread soft fruit onto blunt reusable skewers while younger children can make fruit patterns on a plate instead.

10. Decorated rice-cereal treats

Use prepared cereal treats, cut them into simple shapes and let the grandkids add a drizzle of melted chocolate and a few sprinkles.

11. Baked quesadilla wedges

Place cheese between tortillas, bake until melted, then cut into wedges. Older kids can help add mild fillings such as beans or cooked chicken.

12. Apple “donut” rings

Core an apple and slice it crosswise into rings. Spread with yogurt, cream cheese or nut/seed butter and decorate with small toppings. Prepare the slicing yourself for younger helpers.

Give each grandchild one predictable kitchen job.

One child can crack eggs, another can pour ingredients, another can set the timer. Repeat the same jobs on future visits and they often become part of the tradition.

What recipes are easiest to make with young grandkids?

No-bake snacks, pancakes, cookies, parfaits and simple pizzas are good starting points because children can help with measuring, stirring, topping or arranging without needing advanced kitchen skills.

How to make cooking with grandkids less stressful

  • Pre-measure anything that is expensive, messy or easy to overpour.
  • Choose one recipe, not an entire meal.
  • Use a large tray or sheet pan under mixing bowls to catch spills.
  • Expect the activity to take longer and build cleanup into the fun.
  • Match tasks to age and supervise around heat, sharp tools and appliances.
